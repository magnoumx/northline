# Competitor matrix

**Research date:** 2026-07-24
**Scope:** Current public web/app product surfaces for Particle, Ground News, Otherweb, Boring Report, Yahoo News, Reuters, AP, BBC, Axios, and The Verge. First-party product, help, standards, subscription, and store pages were preferred.

## Reading notes

- **O — observation:** visible in a current product or stated by the company.
- **I — inference:** an analytical conclusion drawn from those observations.
- **R — recommendation:** what the proposed US news platform should do.
- “Not observed” means it was not evident in the reviewed public surface; it does not prove the feature is absent everywhere.
- Otherweb is a special case: its current domain is an acquisition placeholder. Historical features are separated from its current status.

## Matrix C — competitive judgment

| Competitor | Best feature | Main weakness | Adapt | Improve | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Particle | **I:** The most complete consumer “story object”: cluster + overview + questions + quotes + timeline + media. | **I:** A rich AI layer can feel authoritative before a reader inspects evidence; public questions may add noise and moderation burden. | **R:** Canonical evolving story pages, followed entities/stories, sourced Ask, quotes, timelines, and multiple summary depths. | **R:** Make claim-to-source mapping, confidence, update diffs, editor accountability, and primary-source priority more explicit. | **R:** Do not let conversational fluency substitute for evidence or publish uncited answers. |
| Ground News | **I:** Coverage comparison and Blindspot are useful at the exact point where framing differences matter. | **I:** Outlet-level bias/factuality labels can be mistaken for a verdict on an individual article; important trust metadata is tier-gated. | **R:** Coverage-distribution views, source ownership, headline comparison, and “undercovered by” signals. | **R:** Explain methodology inline, separate article evidence from outlet priors, show unrated coverage, and keep core provenance free. | **R:** Avoid a single left/right score as a proxy for truth or a politically loaded “unbiased” claim. |
| Otherweb | **I:** Historical emphasis on low-noise controls and explainable filtering remains directionally useful. | **I:** There is no current usable product to benchmark, and prior scale/quality claims cannot validate present capability. | **R:** Only the general principle of explicit filtering controls. | **R:** Validate every revived idea through current user testing and current operational evidence. | **R:** Do not cite historical press claims as a live competitive feature or depend on the company’s current availability. |
| Boring Report | **I:** A calm, minimal reading experience lowers the emotional temperature of the feed. | **I:** “Boring” wording is not an accuracy system; the synthetic summary lacks claim-level support, uncertainty, and editorial identity. | **R:** Calm typography, short neutral orientation, source list, compact feed, and optional streaks. | **R:** Add bylines/editors, evidence links per claim, update/correction history, known/unknown, and a disclosed methodology. | **R:** Avoid marketing “neutral” or “unbiased” as proof and avoid rewriting into a source-substituting article. |
| Yahoo News | **I:** Distribution, publisher breadth, account-based controls, comments, alerts, and habit loops operate at mass-market scale. | **I:** Dense ads and mixed-quality publisher content can obscure hierarchy and provenance. | **R:** Strong onboarding preferences, block controls, saves/history, morning briefing, publisher/author follow, and search. | **R:** Reduce ad noise, rank provenance visibly, label AI takeaways, and show why an item is recommended. | **R:** Avoid ad-like cards that resemble editorial content or automatic summaries detached from sources. |
| Reuters | **I:** Fast global/markets reporting with disciplined attribution, datelines, and correction standards. | **I:** Utilitarian breadth and markets-first density are less approachable for casual readers; no cross-publisher synthesis. | **R:** Attribution discipline, correction transparency, professional live updates, strong photo/graphic desks, and market/event watchlists. | **R:** Add optional plain-language orientation, topic timelines, and source/document panels without diluting original reporting. | **R:** Avoid copying wire text or imagery without a license; attribution alone is not permission. |
| AP | **I:** Original reporting plus photography, explicit updated time, and large evolving topic packages. | **I:** The very long, content-rich homepage can be difficult to scan; cluster-level perspective comparison is absent. | **R:** Visible update timestamps, visual provenance, “More Coverage” packages, follow authors/topics, short formats, and accessibility options. | **R:** Create a more compact personal briefing layer and explicit change history on developing stories. | **R:** Avoid assuming a free consumer page grants reuse rights to AP text or media. |
| BBC | **I:** Strong blend of live, audio, video, explainers, Verify, and international depth within a calm hierarchy. | **I:** Broad global scope can dilute US-local relevance; ads/subscription prompts add friction in the US. | **R:** Live hubs, explainers, verification vertical, audio background play, most-watched/read, and restrained modular hierarchy. | **R:** Localize US relevance and let users choose text/audio/video intensity and notification cadence. | **R:** Avoid importing public-service brand cues or relying on a one-size-fits-all global agenda. |
| Axios | **I:** Best-in-class scannability and explicit “why it matters” structure. | **I:** A rigid brevity formula can flatten uncertainty, causal complexity, and contested interpretation. | **R:** Semantic labels, one-sentence relevance, disciplined paragraph length, and newsletter/local habit loops. | **R:** Pair every brief with expandable evidence, chronology, counterevidence, methodology, and depth. | **R:** Avoid turning every story into the same rhetorical template or confusing brevity with completeness. |
| The Verge | **I:** Strongest visual/editorial identity and best fast/deep format range among the set. | **I:** Bold mosaics can be cognitively dense; opinionated voice and commerce require conspicuous labels. | **R:** Distinct templates for quick posts/news/reviews/features, expressive but accessible art direction, primary-link posts, clear corrections, and full-text RSS. | **R:** Carry the visual confidence into a calmer general-news system with stricter news/analysis/opinion/commerce separation. | **R:** Avoid copying its trade dress, type treatment, color system, or specialist voice; avoid ambiguous affiliate/sponsor presentation. |

## Bottom line

**I:** No reviewed product combines all four of these at once: (1) wire-grade human accountability, (2) a living multi-source story object, (3) claim-level evidence and uncertainty, and (4) a calm, US/local-aware personalized experience. Particle comes closest on interaction design, Ground News on coverage comparison, Reuters/AP/BBC on newsroom trust, Axios on compression, and The Verge on format/identity. The opportunity is to combine those strengths without inheriting outlet-score overreach, synthetic source substitution, ad clutter, or unsupported claims of neutrality.
