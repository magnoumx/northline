# Corrections Policy

**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-24

## Commitment

We correct factual and contextual errors promptly, visibly, and
proportionately. We preserve publication history and do not quietly rewrite a
material mistake.

Before public launch, the operating publisher must place a monitored correction
email/form on the public corrections page and state expected response hours.

## How to report a concern

A report should include the article URL, disputed passage, reason, and any
supporting evidence. We do not require a reader to disclose more personal data
than needed to respond. Good-faith reports are not penalized.

Reports enter a tracked queue with receipt time, affected version, severity,
owner, evidence, decision, action, and completion time. Confidential-source and
security reports use restricted handling.

## Types of change

| Type | Example | Public treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor edit | Typo or grammar that does not change meaning | Create a successor version; no public note normally required |
| Clarification | Wording was accurate but reasonably ambiguous/incomplete | Material clarification note when reader understanding changes |
| Correction | Factual error in name, number, date, quote, context, headline, media, or implication | New immutable version and visible correction note |
| Update | New material information after accurate publication | Timestamped update note; not labeled a correction unless prior text was wrong |
| Retraction | Central premise unsupported/false or integrity cannot be repaired | Retraction notice at canonical URL; distribution halted/corrected |
| Removal/410 | Exceptional legal, privacy, safety, or duplicate case | Authorized reason, preserved internal record, appropriate status/redirect |

Cosmetic edits do not change the modified date.

## Triage

1. Preserve the exact published version and report.
2. Assess ongoing harm, centrality, sensitivity, distribution, and legal/security
   implications.
3. Freeze scheduled promotion, alerts, newsletters, or automation when needed.
4. Assign an editor independent enough to investigate.
5. Recheck primary and independent evidence.
6. Contact affected parties when appropriate.
7. Decide and record action.

Critical factual errors, dangerous emergency information, false death reports,
wrong-person identification, unsupported allegations, and exposed private
information receive immediate escalation.

## Correction note

A correction note states:

- what was wrong;
- the correct information;
- where the error appeared when useful; and
- when the correction was made.

It does not obscure responsibility with vague wording such as “updated for
clarity” when the previous text was false. It avoids unnecessarily repeating
harmful private or defamatory text.

The corrected page shows the current version, publication time, meaningful
modified time, and correction history. Internal records preserve previous
versions, approvers, evidence, and downstream actions.

## Headlines, media, and structured data

A correction covers every representation of the error:

- on-site/SEO/social/push/newsletter headline;
- subheadline, key facts, captions, alt text, transcript, chart, and timeline;
- canonical structured data;
- thumbnail/hero media;
- Story Intelligence summary and Ask This Story dossier; and
- related internal links/snippets.

Changing the body alone is insufficient.

## Downstream correction

After a material correction:

- invalidate caches;
- update search projection and on-site recommendations;
- update feeds/sitemaps/structured data where applicable;
- append the Story Intelligence history;
- correct pending newsletters/notifications;
- send a follow-up to already-sent channels when harm/importance warrants;
- notify syndication or licensed partners where contractually supported; and
- review other articles using the same claim/source/model output.

External caches cannot always be changed immediately; the reconciliation job
records attempts.

## Retractions

Retraction is appropriate when the central claim is false or unsupported,
fabrication/plagiarism compromises the work, the wrong person/event was
identified, or repair would leave a misleading artifact.

The canonical URL normally remains with:

- the original headline or a safe identifying title;
- a prominent `RETRACTED` label;
- a concise explanation and effective time;
- removal of unsafe unsupported body/media when necessary; and
- links to corrected coverage where available.

Retraction is authorized by the Editor-in-Chief or documented emergency
delegate and triggers a retrospective.

## Removal and legal/privacy requests

We do not remove accurate reporting merely because it is inconvenient.
Requests involving court outcomes, expungement, minors, victims, doxxing,
copyright, safety, or law receive case-specific editorial/legal review.
Options include correction, update, deindexing, redaction, media replacement,
limited access, redirect, or `410`.

Internal evidence and audit retention remain subject to law, safety, and
privacy—not public convenience.

## Live coverage

Live entries are versioned individually. Correct the entry, add a note at or
near it, update any summary that inherited the error, and do not silently change
the original event time. A rolling feed does not exempt old entries from
correction.

## AI-assisted content

AI errors receive the same public correction treatment. Internally, serious
cases also pause the prompt/model/agent as appropriate, preserve redacted
evidence, inspect related output, and require reevaluation before re-enable.

## Targets

These are service targets, not promises that bypass investigation:

- acknowledge ordinary reports within one business day;
- triage critical active harm within 30 minutes during staffed coverage;
- publish a verified correction as soon as the correct wording and approval are
  ready; and
- keep reporters informed when a complex investigation takes longer.

## Accountability

Correction rate is a learning measure, not a target to suppress. Retrospectives
look for source, workflow, interface, staffing, model, and policy improvements.
We protect correction records from unauthorized alteration.
