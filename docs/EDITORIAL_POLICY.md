# Editorial Policy

**Effective for:** all editorial formats, headlines, alerts, newsletters, audio,
social copy, visuals, and Story Intelligence content
**Last reviewed:** 2026-07-24

## Mission

We help a United States audience understand important events with accurate,
calm, useful, and transparent reporting. We publish fewer, stronger stories
rather than filling a quota.

Every published story must add at least two meaningful forms of original value,
such as multi-source synthesis, a verified timeline, historical context, a
primary document, a data or comparison module, geographic context, an
explanation of disagreement, why the event matters, or what happens next. If we
cannot add meaningful value, we do not publish.

## Editorial authority

Human editors are accountable for publication. AI systems may help discover,
organize, extract, compare, draft, or review material, but they do not establish
facts, create permission, or waive policy.

Commercial staff, advertisers, sponsors, analytics, search demand, and model
scores do not determine factual conclusions. Sponsored content is produced and
labeled separately.

## Accuracy and verification

- The first two paragraphs state what happened in plain language.
- Important claims are linked internally to supporting evidence.
- We prefer primary records and seek multiple independent credible sources.
- A source repeating another report is not independent confirmation.
- Social posts, viral media, tips, and trend signals are leads, not proof.
- Dates, names, titles, numbers, locations, quotations, and media context receive
  explicit checks.
- We distinguish fact, allegation, estimate, analysis, opinion, and unknown.
- We do not smooth over conflicting credible reports.
- Missing reliable support blocks publication.

Central claims marked unverified, false, or conflicting cannot be published as
fact. A story may accurately report that credible accounts conflict when the
conflict itself is the subject and is clearly labeled.

## Source transparency

We link to primary records and supporting reporting when lawful and useful. We
identify the source of material close to the claim. The source list does not
replace inline attribution.

Anonymous or confidential sources are exceptional. An authorized editor must
know the source's identity, assess motive and access, corroborate important
claims, explain anonymity to the reader as specifically as safety permits, and
record the decision securely.

We do not display political-bias or factuality ratings unless a qualified
third-party methodology is licensed, attributed, current, and appropriately
explained. Automated ideology labels never decide which position is true.

See [SOURCE_POLICY.md](./SOURCE_POLICY.md).

## Content labels

Readers must know what kind of work they are reading:

| Type | Required treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Reported news | Named accountable byline, direct/original reporting basis, sourcing, and review |
| Synthesis | Clear synthesis label or equivalent; identified source set; no implication of firsthand reporting |
| Live coverage | Entry timestamps, byline/desk, source, verification state, and visible corrected entries |
| Explainer | Scope, sources, last-reviewed date, and stale-fact review trigger |
| Analysis | `ANALYSIS` before click and on page; evidence, expertise, and material counterevidence |
| Opinion | `OPINION` before click and on page; affiliation/conflicts; factual corrections still apply |
| Review | Method, test conditions, affiliate/commercial disclosure, and no advertiser approval |
| Sponsored | Unambiguous paid label, separate template/workflow, and no imitation of independent news |
| Material AI assistance | Proportionate disclosure, human owner, and source support |

We do not label work “unbiased” or claim that calm wording is itself a
verification method.

## Fairness and right of response

Coverage should represent material context and credible disagreement without
creating false equivalence. When a story contains a serious allegation or may
substantially harm a person's reputation, we make a reasonable effort to seek a
response, state whether and when one was requested, and update fairly if a
response arrives.

We avoid loaded language, unsupported superlatives, false urgency, emotional
manipulation, and headlines that outrun the evidence. We do not exploit tragedy
or conceal essential context for clicks.

## Sensitive coverage

The following always require human approval:

- U.S. elections, government leadership, and presidential coverage;
- war, military action, and terrorism;
- crime, accusations, lawsuits, and leaked private information;
- deaths, missing persons, minors, sexual misconduct, and identity claims;
- health, public health, investments, and personal finance;
- natural disasters and active emergencies;
- graphic or traumatic events; and
- stories likely to damage a person's reputation.

Urgency changes priority, not verification. Headlines, hero media, alerts,
social copy, and newsletters are part of the approval package. Material changes
after approval require a new review.

### Elections

Use official election authorities and qualified results providers. Distinguish
projections from certified results, name the source and timestamp, and avoid
declaring outcomes before the applicable standard is met. Voting logistics
require current official sourcing.

### Crime and allegations

Use precise legal language, include procedural status and response, avoid
implying guilt, protect minors and victims as policy/law requires, and revisit
coverage when a case materially changes.

### Health and finance

Separate general reporting from personal advice, describe evidence quality and
limitations, cite primary/regulatory material where available, disclose
conflicts, and avoid guaranteed outcomes.

### Disasters and emergencies

Prioritize official safety information, timestamps, location, uncertainty, and
accessible instructions. Do not use stale or unrelated dramatic media.

## Writing and headlines

The voice is direct, clear, credible, modern, calm, informative, and natural
American English.

Avoid empty openings, keyword stuffing, fake quotations, repeated summaries,
formulaic rhetorical questions, sensational labels, unsupported certainty, and
AI filler. A headline must accurately match the article and must not promise
photos, video, certainty, or a consequence the story does not deliver.

SEO, homepage, social, newsletter, and push headlines can differ, but every
variant must remain accurate and preserve essential context.

## Bylines, dates, and disclosures

Published pages show:

- accountable author/byline;
- editor or specialist reviewer where materially relevant;
- publication date/time and timezone context;
- modified date only for a meaningful update;
- source and media attribution;
- correction/update history; and
- material AI-assistance disclosure under [AI_POLICY.md](./AI_POLICY.md).

Cosmetic changes do not reset freshness.

## Media

Media must be relevant, accurately captioned, rights-cleared, and not
manipulatively cropped. File/archive/illustration/AI labels are used where
needed. AI-generated imagery never represents itself as documentary evidence.
See [MEDIA_RIGHTS.md](./MEDIA_RIGHTS.md).

## Updates, corrections, and retractions

We correct errors promptly and proportionally, preserve prior published
versions, and make material correction notes visible. We do not quietly
overwrite a significant error. Retractions normally keep the canonical page
with an explanation. See [CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md](./CORRECTIONS_POLICY.md).

## Conflicts of interest

Authors and editors disclose relevant financial, personal, employment, family,
political, gift, travel, or sponsor relationships to an editor. The editor may
reassign, add disclosure, narrow the work, or decline coverage.

Staff do not accept payment for favorable editorial coverage. Review products,
travel support, embargo access, or affiliate relationships are disclosed and do
not guarantee coverage or a positive conclusion.

## Advertising and sponsored content

Advertising is clearly labeled and visually separated from editorial content.
Sponsors do not preview or approve independent editorial conclusions. Sponsored
content uses a distinct workflow and disclosure. See
[ADVERTISING.md](./ADVERTISING.md).

## Reader personalization

Readers may follow or block subjects, but a non-personalized public-interest Top
Stories layer remains. Personalization does not change an article's facts,
verification, or correction history.

Alerts about a followed story should identify a meaningful verified change
rather than repeat the same headline. Recommendation surfaces explain why an
item appears and provide controls; the newsroom, not an engagement model, owns
the essential-news layer.

## Complaints and accountability

The public site must provide a monitored corrections/editorial contact before
launch and name the operating publisher, ownership, and funding. Reports are
logged, acknowledged, investigated against the exact version, and protected
from retaliation. We publish aggregate correction/accountability information
when practical without exposing confidential sources or reader data.

## Demo/reference content

Development content is fictional and visibly marked. It must not invent current
breaking news or fabricated scandals involving real people. Demo metrics,
sources, workflows, and AI results are never represented as live operations.

## Enforcement

The Editor-in-Chief owns this policy. Violations may pause publication, disable
automation, trigger correction/retraction, restrict access, or initiate an
incident review. Exceptions require a documented authorized decision and can
never waive law, central-claim support, media rights, or required sensitive
review.
